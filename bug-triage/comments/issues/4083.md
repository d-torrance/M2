Closing as a duplicate of **#4027**, which reports the same defect through `WeylClosure` rather
than `Dintegration` and is two months older. Your first bullet is right, and the shared cause can
be pinned down exactly.

Both issues die at the same place — `BernsteinSato/bFunction.ideal.m2:228`, through the same
frames — and instrumenting `factorBFunction` to print what it is handed shows they are handed the
*same polynomial*:

```
#4027 (WeylClosure)   b-function: s^3-s^2-s     factors: (s)*(s^2-s-1)
#4083 (Dintegration)  b-function: s^3-s^2-s     factors: (s)*(s^2-s-1)
```

which is the `s(s^2-s-1)` you named here. `factorBFunction` factors the b-function over `ZZ` and
then requires every irreducible factor to be linear:

```m2
result := apply(f, u->(
          if first degree u#0 != 1 then error "internal error: incorrect b-function";
```

`s^2-s-1` is irreducible over `QQ` — its roots are the golden ratio and its conjugate — so it has
degree 2 and the check raises. Any b-function with an irrational root fails here, whatever calls
it.

**One line clears both.** Replacing that `error` with `continue`, so non-linear factors are
skipped, makes both issues return:

```
#4027   WeylClosure I    -->  ideal(x^2*dx^2-2*x*dx+1, -x^2*dx^2+2*x*dx-1, 2*x^2*dx^3-2*dx)
#4083   Dintegration...  -->  cokernel matrix {{x^2*dx^2+2*x*dx-1}}
```

Skipping is at least defensible for `getIntRoots`, which wants integer roots: a factor irreducible
over `QQ` of degree greater than 1 has no rational roots, so it contributes none.

**But I have not checked that either answer is correct, and that is the open question**, not the
error. It is your "or something else": if a b-function with irrational roots means the hypotheses
of the algorithm have failed, then returning a value silently is worse than raising, and the right
fix is a better diagnostic rather than a skip. Someone who knows the D-module theory needs to
decide which — the change above is offered as a way to see both symptoms disappear at once, not as
a patch.

That question moves to #4027 along with this close.
