Closing: your diagnosis was right, and this turns out to be a bdwgc bug rather than an M2 one.

[bdwgc#324](https://github.com/ivmai/bdwgc/issues/324) was fixed and closed upstream on 2021-06-10.
GitHub's compare API places the fix commit inside `v8.2.0` and outside `v8.0.6`, so every 8.2.x has
it and the whole 8.0.x line — including the `libgc.so.1.4.3` in your trace — does not. Updating
libgc, as you said, is the fix.

Most M2 installations get gc from the distribution rather than from M2: the Debian/Ubuntu and
Homebrew packages, the RPM build, and CI all pass `--with-system-gc`. So for nearly everyone this
was resolved by their distribution moving past bdwgc 8.2.0 — Ubuntu 24.04, for instance, ships
8.2.6.

For the minority who build M2's own vendored copy, that moved too: it was 8.0.4 when you filed, and
[PR #3289](https://github.com/Macaulay2/M2/pull/3289) took it to 8.2.6 in June 2024, with later
bumps to 8.2.12.

If you still see `mmap(PROT_NONE) ... ENOMEM`, the thing to check is which libgc the binary is
linked against — a package built against an 8.0.x system libgc would still show this.
