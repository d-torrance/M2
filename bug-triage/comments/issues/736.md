### Why this is not #607

The two matrices in the report encode the same map, multiplication by `x`, in two different
ways:

| | map degree | source degrees | homogeneous |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `f = matrix {{x}}` | `{0}` | `{{1}}` | yes — grading carried in the source |
| `g = x * id_(R^1)` | `{1}` | `{{0}}` | yes — grading carried in the map degree |

A matrix carries **one** map degree for all its columns, so `f|g` would need degree 0 for its
first column and 1 for its second. Nothing was computed and lost here; no single degree exists.
The only route to a homogeneous answer is to re-encode one operand by twisting its source, which
is what @rz10x suggested in 2019.

The contrast is visible between the two operands of this very example:

```m2
i1 : R = QQ[x]; f = matrix {{x}}; g = x * id_(R^1);

i4 : degree (f|f), isHomogeneous (f|f)

o4 = ({0}, true)

i5 : degree (g|g), isHomogeneous (g|g)

o5 = ({0}, false)
```

`g|g` is #607 — both operands have degree `{1}`, the result should too, and it is discarded.
`f|g` is this issue. One of Dan's own two matrices demonstrates each.

### The four issues, and why they should not be merged

| | issues | what is wrong | what a fix does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | #607 (`\|`, `\|\|`), #1060 (`++`) | operands share a degree, result comes back `{0}` | keep the degree that is already computed |
| **B** | **#736** (`\|`, mixed encodings), #1031 (`matrix` of vectors) | no single map degree exists | assign source degrees per column |

Class A is one defect across three operators — `degree (f\|f)`, `degree (f\|\|f)` and
`degree (f++f)` are all `{0}` for `f` of degree `{1}` — and #607, as the oldest, is the natural
home for it.

The reason the two classes cannot be collapsed is that they put the grading in **different
places**. A class B fix assigns it to the source; applied to #607's `m|m` that would make the
result homogeneous while leaving `degree (m|m)` at `{0}` — satisfying the symptom while missing
the ask, since #607 says explicitly that it should be `{2}`. So a single ticket covering all
four would be fixed twice over and still be half wrong.
