Still true on 1.26.06-40, with numbers, from a sweep of the open issues. Nobody has
confirmed this since it was filed, and the figures here were from a machine called
"habanero" in 2014.

What makes this measurable rather than a vague complaint is that the report carries its own
control: the same shape of workload with and without allocation. Four tasks, `allowableThreads = 5`:

| workload | one alone | four in parallel | speedup vs 4× serial |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `spin` — burns CPU, allocates nothing | 7s | 7s | **4.0×** (perfect) |
| `for i to 30000000 do ()` — makes garbage | 4s | 9s | **1.78×** |

```m2
spin = value Core#"private dictionary"#"spin";
alloc   = () -> (for i to 30000000 do (); 1);
noalloc = () -> (spin 20000; 1);
four = fn -> (t := currentTime();
    s := for i from 1 to 4 list schedule fn;
    while (not all(s, isReady)) do sleep 1;
    currentTime() - t);
```

The non-allocating tasks parallelise perfectly, so this is not a scheduling problem, not a
thread-count problem, and not "the machine is busy" — the four threads plainly do run at
once when the work does not allocate. Only the allocating case loses the parallelism, which
is what this issue said in 2014.

### Where the mechanism is written down

This looks like the performance-side symptom of what #175 describes:

> Some of our code and many of our third party libraries are not thread safe, and some of
> those that are, such as mpfr, achieve thread safety in a way that makes our use of libgc
> break, by storing libgc pointers in thread local variables in locations unknown to the
> libgc garbage collector.

#175 is open and carries a checklist from @mahrud dated 2025-07-05.

The crash-side symptom of the same collision was #151 — a nondeterministic SIGSEGV and
libgc `Duplicate large block deallocation` during NumericalAlgebraicGeometry examples.
That one has just been closed as not reproducible: 57 runs of the package's test suite on
1.26.06 produced no crash signatures, and the mpfr version Dan blamed for it (3.1.2 versus
3.0.1) is long gone. This issue is the half of that story that did *not* go away, and it
still measures.
