Timings, since @mikestillman asked for them — and they suggest a third of the cost can go without
any engine work.

Measured on 1.26.06 over `kk = ZZ/101`, `R = kk[x_0..x_10]`, with the `exponentMatrix` from the
first post:

| | columns | `basis` | `exponentMatrix` | ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `basis(6, R)` | 8,008 | 0.008s | 0.376s | 45× |
| `basis(8, R)` | 43,758 | 0.042s | 2.00s | 47× |

So the ratio is stable and roughly linear in the number of columns rather than degrading. My absolute
figures are about an order of magnitude below the ones described above — a couple of seconds for the
basis, a minute for the exponents — but the *ratio* is the same, which is the part that was in
question.

**Where the 2 seconds go.** There is no engine routine that takes a matrix of monomials to a matrix
of exponents; `exponents RingElement` goes through `listForm`, which is one `rawPairs` call per
element followed by `exponents(ZZ, RawMonomial)` building an `nvars`-long list. Two pieces of that
are avoidable from the top level:

```
transpose matrix apply(numcols B, c -> first exponents B_(0,c))        2.00 s
transpose matrix apply(first entries B, m -> first exponents m)        1.81 s
-- and skipping the coefficient promotion that "first \ listForm" discards:
transpose matrix apply(first entries B, m -> (
        (cc, mm) := rawPairs(raw kk, raw m);
        exponents(numgens R, mm#0)))                                   1.34 s
```

All three agree on the answer. The middle line avoids a per-column submatrix extraction, worth about
10%. The last avoids `listForm` pairing each exponent vector with a `promote`d coefficient that
`first \` then throws away — on a matrix of monomials that is pure overhead, and it is worth another
23%.

So about a third of the cost is recoverable without touching the engine, which may or may not settle
whether the engine routine is wanted. The remaining ~1.3s is one `rawPairs` and one 11-element list
per monomial, 43,758 times.
